# Code of conduct

## The short version

Be decent to people. Assume the person you are replying to is acting in good faith
and is probably missing context rather than being stupid. If you would not say it to
a colleague standing next to you, don't type it into an issue.

## What this covers

Issues, pull requests, discussions, commit messages, code review, and any other
space where people are working on Tastatur.

## Expected behaviour

- Give people the benefit of the doubt, particularly across a language barrier or a
  timezone. Written English flattens tone, and a message that reads as curt is
  usually just short.
- Criticise the code, not the person who wrote it. "This has a race condition when
  two requests arrive in the same second" is useful. "This is amateur work" is not,
  and it is also less informative.
- Say why, not only what. A review comment that explains the failure mode teaches
  something; one that just says "change this" has to be taken on authority.
- Accept that a maintainer may decline your change. Sometimes a patch is good and
  still not a fit. That is not a judgement of you or of the work.
- Accept that a contributor may not have time to finish something. Nobody here owes
  anyone else their evenings.

## Unacceptable behaviour

Harassment, personal attacks, and demeaning or discriminatory comments. Sexual
attention or imagery in project spaces. Publishing private information without
consent. Sustained disruption of discussions. Attempting to route around a
maintainer's decision by pressure, repetition, or bringing it to another venue to
relitigate.

Deliberately introducing insecure or malicious code is a special case, and it is
handled as an immediate and permanent ban rather than as a matter for discussion.
Given what this project does, so is the deliberate weakening of a privacy invariant
disguised as a refactor.

## Reporting

Email <conduct@reedster.llc>. Reports go to Reedster LLC and are handled privately.

Include whatever you have — links, dates, and what you would like to see happen. You
are not required to have a solution in mind, and you are not required to have already
tried to resolve it yourself.

You will get a reply within **5 working days**. If the report concerns a maintainer,
say so, and it will be handled by someone else where the project's size allows and
with a disclosed conflict of interest where it does not. Tastatur is a small project
maintained by one company; pretending otherwise would be misleading, so instead the
commitment is transparency about who is deciding.

Reporting something in good faith will not be held against you, and retaliation for
a report is itself a violation.

## Consequences

Depending on severity: a private word, a public correction, a temporary ban, or a
permanent one. Where there is room for interpretation, the first step is usually a
conversation rather than a sanction, because most conduct problems in a project this
size are misunderstandings and are fixable by talking.

Decisions are made by the maintainers and are final within this project.

## Scope

Project spaces, plus public spaces when someone is representing Tastatur. Behaviour
elsewhere is generally not our business, with one exception: conduct that gives
contributors a well-founded reason to feel unsafe working with someone here is
relevant wherever it happened.
